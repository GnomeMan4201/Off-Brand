Station standards

The jokes are satire. The evidence has to stand on its own.

Off-Brand is an editorial screenshot zine, not a phishing detection service. A reported address is a research lead, not a verdict. We examine archived material and explain what it can and cannot establish.

01 // FROM THE CONTROL ROOM

How a review comes together

  1. Collect

    Use saved public scan records and archived screenshots from permitted sources. An intake report is a lead; the collector does not visit the reported target.

  2. Assess

    Keep exact URL matches separate from related addresses, record source availability and avoid counting shared origins as independent evidence.

  3. Write

    Separate visible observations, source-attributed findings, plausible interpretations and unknowns. Add a practical reader lesson. Keep satire separate from factual claims.

  4. Review

    New publication requires authenticated collection evidence, an explicitly selected owner-approved review and a publishable evidence level. Provider reuse restrictions also apply.

Collection authentication protects the recorded issue body against edits and cross-issue reuse. It does not prove the provider's claims are correct.

02 // THE EVIDENCE LOCKER

What the ratings mean

Corroborated
Reserved for demonstrably independent supporting evidence. The current sources do not award this level.
Source-confirmed
A verifying source has confirmed the exact URL as phishing. This is an attributed finding, not a guarantee of current behavior or operator identity.
Suspected (not confirmed)
Supporting leads exist, but the exact URL has not been verified. A source tag, automated listing or related address is not confirmation.
Unverified
No supporting record was established in the available checks. This is not a finding that the page is safe. New publication is blocked.
Disputed
Records conflict with the classification. New publication is blocked until resolved.
Not yet rated
A historical review predates structured evidence ratings. It has not been independently confirmed under the current method simply because it is on the shelf.

An HTTP/HTTPS-only difference is related, not exact. A match on the same host is also not exact confirmation. An unavailable or skipped source is not a negative result; historical verification and current source health are separate.

03 // NO SIGNAL IS STILL NO SIGNAL

Sources and limitations

urlscan.io

Archived screenshots, discovery and recorded scan metadata. Submitter tags and automated verdicts are not independent proof. Full result records may be unavailable without authenticated access.

PhishTank

Verified, exact-page records can support source confirmation. The current online feed is incomplete for historical cases: an absent listing does not establish that a page was benign.

Restricted sources

OpenPhish is limited to authorized private research; its restricted records are not included in public evidence. Unknown upstream relationships are not treated as independence.

A screenshot shows one captured state. It cannot establish where a form sends information, whether a wallet transaction occurred, how a redirect is decided, or who operates a site. Domain age, hosting platforms, language and security badges are clues, not verdicts.

Public availability does not automatically permit redistribution. New public intake and publishing are gated on documented reuse authorization; historical material remains subject to rights review. Read the source notes and licensing limitations.

04 // CORRECTIONS DESK

When we get something wrong

A correction is part of the record, not a reason to hide it. Our editorial standard is to distinguish a changed claim from a changed confidence rating, and to record what changed, why, when and what evidence supports the change.

Public correction log

No individual review corrections are recorded on this page at launch. This is not a claim that every existing review has been verified or is error-free. Historical reviews still require source-level reassessment.

When an approved correction is published, this log should identify the review, previous claim, corrected claim, date and basis without disclosing restricted records or sensitive information.

Report an error or rights concern

Use the repository's issue form to identify the channel or review, describe the concern and explain the requested correction. Issues are public. Do not include credentials, private data, active suspicious links, restricted material or sensitive rights documentation. Request a private contact route if needed.

For a correction request, the useful details are the review name, the specific wording at issue, and a safe reference or description of the evidence. Do not interact with a suspicious page to prove a claim.
